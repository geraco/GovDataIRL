86% of Irish bathing sites achieved excellent water quality in 2025, with Mayo accounting for 28% of all monitored beaches

Why this matters

This follows the EPA's May 2026 report highlighting that heavy rainfall events became more frequent during the 2025 bathing season, with bathing water warnings nearly doubling. Urban wastewater issues were the most frequently reported cause of bathing water problems, leading to temporary beach closures and restrictions. The data shows which beaches maintained safe swimming conditions and where infrastructure pressures emerged during a challenging season.

Related: High bathing water quality maintained in 2025, despite increased pressure from heavy rainfall | Environmental Protection Agency, EPA publishes Bathing Water Quality in Ireland report for 2025 - Catchments.ie - Catchments.ie, 2025 Bathing Water Quality Report - Beaches.ie - Beaches.ie
  • 86% of monitored bathing sites (43 of 50) achieved the highest 'Excellent Quality' classification in 2025, down from 92% the previous year
  • Water quality has been consistently strong over the past four years, with 'Excellent Quality' classifications ranging from 90% in 2022 and 2023 to 92% in 2024
  • Mayo dominates the dataset with 28% of all monitored sites (14 of 50), followed by Galway with 13 sites, suggesting these western counties have particularly extensive coastal and lake bathing areas
  • 46 of the 50 sites (92%) are identified as having short-term pollution risk, indicating most beaches require active management despite their generally excellent long-term water quality ratings
  • 68% of sites (34 of 50) have achieved Blue Flag status, while less than a fifth (9 of 50) have Green Coast accreditation, showing strong amenity provision alongside environmental quality
A caveat worth knowing: This dataset represents only 50 identified bathing locations and excludes any sites that may have been monitored but did not meet the criteria for inclusion, so it cannot tell us about overall national bathing water coverage or sites that may have been removed from monitoring
