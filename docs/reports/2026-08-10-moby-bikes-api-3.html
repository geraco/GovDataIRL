Nearly all Moby bikes are in standard service, but one in five locations show anomalous GPS readings

Why this matters

This follows reporting last year of thousands of fines issued to Moby users for abandoned or badly parked bikes, and complaints from Dublin councillors about bikes being "discarded constantly". Anomalous GPS readings could make it harder for the company to track down misplaced bikes or for users to reliably locate them through the app, undermining fleet management of the dockless scheme.

  • The fleet is heavily concentrated in one category: 97% of all bike type entries (19,367 of 20,000) are classified as 'DUB-General', with only small shares for 'Workshop' and 'Private' bikes
  • Location data quality is inconsistent: 21.2% of longitude values (4,239 of 20,000) fall outside expected ranges, suggesting GPS tracking issues or bikes moved outside the normal service area
  • Mean battery charge fell 15% over the week from 23 to 29 September 2020, dropping from 70.31% to 59.93%, with the steepest single-day decline of 8% occurring on 26-27 September
  • All bikes in the dataset are electric bikes (20,000 of 20,000 records show 'True' for electric bike status), and none have motor or smart lock features enabled
A caveat worth knowing: This is a snapshot from a one-week period in late September 2020 and may not reflect longer-term fleet composition, seasonal battery performance, or current GPS accuracy
