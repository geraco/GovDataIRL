Nearly all Moby bikes in Dublin were classified as general-use, but one in six location readings fell outside normal geographic bounds

Why this matters

Dockless bike-share services like Moby now operate alongside traditional station-based schemes in Dublin, relying on GPS location data to manage their fleets. Erratic location readings can signal technical problems, bikes being moved or dumped outside permitted areas, or data quality issues that affect operators' ability to rebalance bikes and Dublin City Council's ability to enforce licensing requirements.

  • The 'DUB-General' bike type accounted for 94% of all recorded bike observations (18,826 of 20,000), with only small fractions classified as 'Workshop' or 'Private' use
  • Despite this operational dominance, 15.1% of longitude values (3,017 of 20,000) were statistical outliers, suggesting location-tracking errors or bikes positioned well outside the expected service area
  • Battery levels for general-use bikes totalled 1,236,612 across all observations, 96% higher than the 51,213 recorded for workshop bikes, reflecting the much larger active fleet
  • Average battery charge remained stable across the five-day period at around 67%, peaking at 67.66% on 3 October 2020
A caveat worth knowing: This sample covers only five days in early October 2020 and reflects 20,000 observations from a pool of 94 unique bikes; location outliers may indicate GPS errors, legitimate out-of-area trips, or data quality issues flagged in the publisher's disclaimer about historical files
