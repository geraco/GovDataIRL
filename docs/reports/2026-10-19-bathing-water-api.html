86% of Irish bathing waters achieved Excellent quality, with Mayo hosting over a quarter of all monitored sites

Why this matters

This follows national reporting showing 98% of sites met minimum standards in 2025, but almost half experienced temporary closures or warnings, mainly linked to heavy rainfall and wastewater overflows. Mayo's beaches reported excellent standards, underscoring the county's significance as a key tourism and bathing water destination in the west.

Related: New Dún Laoghaire baths the only 'poor' bathing spot in Ireland last year, Bathing Season Is Back — So How Clean Is the Water really? - Sustainable Water Network, ‘Excellent’ bathing water standards at Mayo beaches
  • Excellent quality accounted for 86% of current annual water quality classifications across all 50 monitored bathing sites, with 92% achieving Excellent in the previous year (2024)
  • Mayo contained 28% of all bathing sites (14 of 50), making it the county with the highest concentration of monitored beaches and lakes
  • Nearly all beaches (46 of 50) were identified as facing short-term pollution risk, indicating vulnerability to temporary water quality deterioration despite the generally high ratings
  • Just 7 sites failed to achieve Excellent quality in 2025: 6 were rated Good and 1 Sufficient, though all remained safe for swimming under EU standards
A caveat worth knowing: This dataset represents locations monitored during the official bathing season (June to mid-September) and does not capture all potential bathing areas in Ireland or year-round water quality conditions
