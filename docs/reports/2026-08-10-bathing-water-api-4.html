Expected duration of bathing restrictions fell 65% between May and August 2026, driven by a shift from algal bloom to rainfall warnings

Why this matters

This follows a summer when heavy rainfall repeatedly closed Dublin beaches, while concerns about toxic algal blooms in midlands lakes also prompted swimming warnings. The shift suggests improved conditions for bathers as the season progressed, though both types of pollution remain ongoing public health concerns around Irish bathing waters.

  • Mayo County Council accounted for 71% of all active bathing restrictions (5 of 7), with all five Mayo incidents lasting 5 days and linked to heavy rainfall warnings
  • The single longest restriction—14 days at Keeldra Lough in Leitrim—was caused by algal bloom rather than rainfall, and accounted for 180% more total restricted days than any other incident
  • Six of the seven incidents were about potential water quality deterioration due to expected heavy rainfall (five prior warnings and one bathing prohibition), while only one was a full bathing prohibition due to algal bloom
