National Beef Kill Figures 2023

Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine cc-by Data covers 2019–2023 · dataset listing last updated 2024-03-06T13:50:58.257372

Beef slaughter rose 18% between 2019 and 2023, driven by a sharp 19% surge in 2020 before levelling off

Why this matters

The 2020 surge followed disruptions to abattoir operations from COVID-19 restrictions in Q2, with much of the annual rise occurring in Q3 when throughputs rebounded from 2019's farmer protests at processing plants over beef prices. Ireland's beef sector is a critical export industry, with strong export performance reported in recent years despite ongoing debates about climate targets and herd reduction.

Related: Irish beef: what's the current picture, and the outlook for 2021? | AHDB
  • Average weekly slaughter numbers increased from 4,077 in late 2019 to 4,797 by late 2023, an 18% rise over the four-year period
  • The sharpest year-on-year increase was 19% in 2020, followed by a 5% decline in 2021, an 8% rise in 2022, and a 4% decline in 2023
  • Steers accounted for 2.37 million animals slaughtered over the period, 27% more than heifers (1.73 million), making them the dominant animal type in the beef kill
  • Classification category C (which includes steers) also led with 2.37 million animals, 27% ahead of category E (1.73 million, which includes heifers)
  • Weekly slaughter peaked at an average of 5,001 animals in 2022 before declining 4% in 2023
A caveat worth knowing: This dataset covers weekly beef kill figures but does not explain what drove the 2020 surge or subsequent volatility—factors such as COVID-19 disruptions, market conditions, or herd demographics are not recorded
